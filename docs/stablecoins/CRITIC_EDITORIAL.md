# Critic review: Stablecoins interactive topic (editorial and factual)

Reviewer stance: adversarial. Evidence: R1 to R5 registers. Line numbers refer to the real file lines (`06-stablecoins.ts` = L, `lenses.ts` = LN). Verdicts: S supported, P partly supported, U unsupported by any register claim, C contradicted by the register.

Overall: the page is unusually careful on status words and attribution of vendor numbers. The weaknesses are (a) a few claims that exceed the register's confidence, (b) three places where the page contradicts or silently drops what the register says, (c) provenance caveats that exist in `sources.ts` notes but never reach the reader, and (d) a pro-bank tilt in the "alternatives are closing the gap" lines.

Status-word misuse: `basis: 'rule'` is applied to draft BoE Code of Practice items (LN128, 133, 136, 137) and to the GENIUS interest ban (LN69) that starts 18 Jan 2027. The page promises "Rules are labelled final, draft or proposed" (L267 area) but the lens collapses all three into `rule`. The matrix row "Issue a sterling stablecoin: Rules final" (L233 area) is true for FCA rules, but the BoE rules in the same cell are draft.

Vendor claims stated as fact: Circle Q3 figures (24), Circle Match (28), KelpDAO cause (31), Citi/HSBC "already run" (41), the Binance freeze narrative (37). Reserves "held by the issuer for holders" for Open USD and USDG (LN253) are vendor statements with no attestation found (ISS-53).

Provenance gap: `SOURCES[*].note` carries "figures from abstract and coverage", "page did not load", "read via secondary", but `cite()` and the "All sources" list render only publisher, date and title. A reader cannot tell the IMF figures come from an abstract, McKinsey from citations, or the PRA letter from Linklaters.

## 2. Acceptance scenarios, from the product alone

**(1) UK business to an Indian bank account, no wallet.** Answered by ch3 (Journey, preset "Bank-account-only supplier"), ch4 and check "india" (LN331). Wallet-to-wallet fails because the route `requires: beneficiary-wallet`: "no address to send to" and the convert-and-withdraw steps fall on the supplier. A fiat payout route needs: a licensed funded off-ramp partner, a destination rail, screening at each hand-off, prefunded local float, and a check that the partner may lawfully pay rupees into the account. Missing: anything about India (RBI/FEMA purpose codes, inward-remittance documentation, tax). The page says so honestly. Real gap: the preset sets `wallet: none` *and* `offramp: none` together, so the stablecoin-provider route is blocked by an assumption the user did not give. Provider-managed routes do not need a supplier wallet. No GBP to INR price evidence (PAY-11 has INR 2.8% RPW for USD-outbound) is used.

**(2) Chain A to Chain B.** ch6 and the lab (`crossLedger.ts`). Contrast is good: recipient holds issuer token vs bridge wrapped token (claim on the bridge, not the issuer) vs provider inventory (counterparty exposure); each shows what it trusts. Partial failure: `dest-fails` on burn-and-mint. Known: burn final, signed message unused, books in transit. Safe: resubmit the same message (one-time nonce). Not safe: a second burn (200,000 for 100,000), marking complete before Chain B finality. `timeout` shows "look up by hash and nonce first". Provider and bridge failures end `at-risk` with no retry. Missing: (a) CCTP Fast Transfer, where attestation precedes source finality and Circle's allowance carries reorg risk (XCH-04, 05; XCH-05 says the loss-bearer is undocumented). The lab always waits for Chain A finality. (b) A timeout on the Chain B submission; `timeout` is source-side only. (c) The "reorg, rebroadcast the same transaction, only one can be included" logic is true for nonce-based chains. Chain A could be Solana, where a blockhash expires. (d) Burn-and-mint exists for USDC only. USDT (61% of supply) has no issuer-native route; USDT0 is a lock-and-mint OFT with an unknown operator (XCH-30). The chapter never says this. (e) `pool-short` models a deposit that then waits, but Stargate V2 reverts at source when path credit is short (XCH-37, inference), so the state may not occur for that design. The "pool's refund process" is unsourced. (f) The blocked-recipient mint failing (XCH-17) is a Med inference from code, presented as fact.

**(3) Fiat by a deadline, issuer window closed.** ch3 steps s3 versus s5 ("The supplier still has nothing"), ch5 and check "deadline". Token settlement is not payout. Options: prefunded local float, sell tokens to a market maker at a spread (3% and capacity-capped when stressed), redeem with the issuer (only if open), credit line, else wait for Monday. Missing: who bears weekend FX and token-price risk while the partner holds tokens; what the sender's contract says if the partner misses the deadline; refund states (CPN has them, XCH-21); and the evidence that the "issuer closed at weekends" assumption is itself contestable. Circle claims around-the-clock mint and redeem via banking partners (XCH-58, Med), Circle Mint accepts RTP, SEPA Instant and PIX 24/7 (XCH-54), and Paxos offers 24/7 conversion (XCH-61). `SLOT_NOTES.issuer` is flagged "assumed", but L193 states "issued against US-dollar bank transfers, which follow banking days" as fact.

**(4) Legitimate payment to unhosted, new or flagged wallet.** ch7 and the wallet cases (LN184 to 220). For each case the scene gives what can be inferred, what cannot, and steps. New wallet: no history is not evidence; hold, confirm address by another channel, proof of control, staged payment. Layering across before, at release and after, with who owns each control. Jurisdiction: UK risk-based, EU ownership check over €1,000. Missing: (a) whether the bank is itself a Travel Rule firm. Part 7A covers crypto exchange and custodian wallet providers (AML-15). A bank paying fiat to a provider is not the same as a bank sending tokens, and the page never separates them. (b) UK reg 64G is firmer than "risk-based": if requested data is not provided the firm "must not make the cryptoasset available" (AML-19). (c) No thresholds for "large" or "higher value". (d) Tipping-off and client communication when holding a payment. (e) The `sanctions` case verdict is "Stop. Don't pay; report", but its own `cannotInfer` says indirect exposure does not show the payee is designated. The verdict should be "Hold and analyse the path" for indirect flags and "Stop" only for listed or reasonably suspected designated control.

**(5) Dollar-stablecoin country vs UK bank.** ch9 and the Lens. The mechanism, measured and scenario labels are the page's best idea. Holder: dollar access, cheaper in some corridors. Bank: deposit funding and lending, tightening domestic credit. Central bank: weaker transmission, imperfect capital controls. Gaps: (a) El Salvador is the only "measured" counter-evidence and it is dollarised. (b) No distinction between substitution of existing FX deposits and new dollar access, which is the IMF's own policy split (PAY-43). (c) The "stablecoin premium" parallel market (PAY-42, PAY-43) is dropped. (d) The UK column is a sterling-regime story, not a UK-bank-versus-dollar-coin story. LN54 labels the issuer tab "US issuer" while the UK cell describes a sterling issuer's 70/30 backing. The prompt says "when dollar stablecoins spread", but the UK cells talk about sterling coins.

**(6) Four models without conflation.** ch8 and `RESERVE_MODELS`. Rows split who owes, backing, who earns, what holder gets, redeem, reach. Check "liability" names issuer, distributor, bank liability and redemption right. Strong. Weaknesses: (a) SoFiUSD is the bank-coin example but is the bank's own issue (44). (b) USDC has two issuers by region (Circle Internet Financial LLC and Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS, ISS-07), so "who owes the holder" depends on the holder's jurisdiction, and the page says "The issuing company". (c) "Distributors and exchanges owe the holder nothing" (LN242) is a legal claim with no source, and is wrong where a platform offers its own rewards or custodial claim. (d) "The banking group keeps the reserve income" (LN264) has no source. (e) Network "Redeem: through the issuer and onboarded partners" (LN255) is unsourced. OUSD minimums and timing are unpublished (ISS-49). (f) "Claim" and "right to redeem" are blurred in the ch1 title and first sentence: for most holders the claim is on terms they cannot enforce directly (Tether's right is "personal to you", XCH-60), while MiCA gives any EMT holder par redemption (REG-47). (g) Tokenised deposits: "deposit protection where eligible" is nearly irrelevant to the wholesale JPM Coin and Citi clients named.

## 3. Balance

Leans towards banks:
- L103: "Instant domestic systems, Swift's faster retail scheme, Nexus (targeted for 2027) and tokenised deposits are closing the gap with central bank money behind them." Nexus is not live (PAY-26). Swift's scheme has no published volumes (PAY-23). FSB shows retail speed KPIs *down* since 2023 (PAY-20). Tokenised deposits are not central bank money.
- L259: Agorá "showed atomic settlement ... with central bank money". A CHF 800,000 test, not integrated with RTGS (PAY-29).
- L253: "Tokenised deposits first: live today, deposit protection, central bank settlement between banks". Closed networks of vetted counterparties (ISS-68), wholesale balances above protection limits, and settlement is only central bank money across banks.
- L214 and LN374: the Binance example omits that about $2.7bn was frozen.
- Journey default fees (journey.ts DEFAULT_FEES, 0.5% FX on every leg) give the correspondent route the same 0.5% FX as a payment firm, while L157 says banks charge 2-4%. Rank order comes from authored leg counts (three fixed fees for the correspondent, two FX legs for the sandwich), which is a modelling choice.

Leans towards stablecoins:
- L96 "case for" Cost bullet: best-case 2% against an all-provider 6.2% average.
- L79 takeaway 4 and L96 "Hours / Reach / Treasury" bullets: three pro-stablecoin claims with no citation attached ("Often they work behind those firms rather than against them" is the page's inference).
- LN262 "Any wallet on supported chains" reach presented as a plain advantage with no mention of Tether's 5-chain redemption closure (XCH-60) or USDC redemption-only chains.
- Missing from ch10 options: no word that CPN is in beta and invitation-only (XCH-22, 23).

## 4. Style

- Em dashes: none in the page prose. Good. Colons are heavy: "Paid means ...", "Mechanisms are ...", "Keep three labels apart:" and many scene fragments. Semicolons are mostly code.
- L227 "lifeline", L260 "The honest answer is conditional", L257 "The risk is quiet": consultancy cadence. Cut "honest", "lifeline" and "quiet".
- L96 / L157: "The G20's 2027 targets ... will be missed" is a pronouncement. Use the FSB wording.
- L80 / L118: "A claim, not a coin" and "A stablecoin is a claim on the company that issued it" overstate for secondary holders and for crypto-collateralised and algorithmic designs the page itself lists next.
- L220: "Getting coins into wallets is the expensive part, so distribution is paid for" is a causal claim from two numbers.
- L118 "That episode cuts both ways: a stablecoin wobbled because a bank failed." Fine, but it reads as a debating point. State the control lesson (concentration of reserve banks).
- A senior reader will challenge: "Large-bank retail wires can carry FX margins of 2-4%" (sample size), "the cheapest traditional provider was cheaper" (cheapest vs cheapest is not the average user), and "Stablecoins win where banks have pulled out" (no evidence offered).
