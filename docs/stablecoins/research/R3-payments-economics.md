# R3 Payments economics: cost, speed, usage and macro evidence

Prepared 18 November 2026 (research run 17–18 Nov 2026) for the Stablecoins topic rebuild. This is research input, not site copy.

Thesis under test: moving a stablecoin token quickly is not the same as completing an international payment.

## 1. Editor summary

- **The IMF "sandwich" paper is a USD 200 study.** WP/26/230 (30 Oct 2026) finds an average all-in cost of 2.18% (median 1.99%, corridor range −2.18% to 8.03%) when users pick the cheapest ramps, 3.04% with median-priced ramps and 4.07% with the most expensive. It also reports USD 50 (4.12%) and USD 5,000 (1.54%). Nothing in it supports a percentage for £1,000 or USD 500. (PAY-01 to PAY-06)
- **The blockchain leg is the cheap part.** Exchange trading fees (0.85 pp) and fiat deposit/withdrawal charges (0.67 pp) dominate the IMF decomposition. BIS WP 1385 finds gas costs of cents and says exchanges now play the correspondent role. Wise's own unit economics put the payment rail at £0.02 of a £0.24 fixed cost per transfer. (PAY-03, PAY-09, PAY-12)
- **Stablecoins are not reliably cheaper.** In about one-third of IMF corridors with comparable data, the cheapest traditional provider beat the cheapest stablecoin route. "Negative cost" corridors reflect stablecoin premiums and capital controls, not efficiency. (PAY-05, PAY-08, PAY-11)
- **Traditional baseline.** The RPW global average for USD 200 was 6.2% in Q1 2026 unadjusted, or 5.7% after FSB rebasing. The FSB says the rebasing explains the entire decline since 2023. Corridor dispersion matters more than the average: US→Mexico 3.7% and US→Philippines 3.4% (RPW medians 2022–25). UK-outbound corridor costs could not be retrieved because the RPW site blocked access. (PAY-11, PAY-14, PAY-15)
- **The G20 2027 targets will be missed.** FSB 2025 said so. FSB 2026 (16 Nov 2026) reports retail costs flat since 2023 (P2P 2.7% and B2B 1.5%, against a 1% target) and progress "gradual and uneven". (PAY-17 to PAY-19)
- **Speed depends on the measurement point.** Swift says 75% of payments reach the beneficiary bank within 10 minutes (operator data, Nov 2026). Only 57% of wholesale cross-border payments are credited end to end within one hour (FSB, Q1 2026), falling to 28.8% for Asia-Pacific and 26% for Africa. The last mile takes 80% of journey time. "SWIFT takes 1–3 days" and "Swift is now instant" are both wrong framings. (PAY-20 to PAY-22)
- **FSB 2026 states the site's thesis in official words.** "Faster payment initiation alone is not sufficient", because end-to-end improvement also depends on FX arrangements, settlement assets, liquidity, legal finality and governance. (PAY-21)
- **Real usage is a small fraction of headline volume.** Gross 2025 stablecoin transactions were USD 28–35tn depending on the source. Genuine payments were about USD 390bn, a single McKinsey/Artemis estimate that BIS and IMF later repeated, so not three independent confirmations. BIS WP 1359 finds about 6 in 10 Ethereum transfer events are misclassified if read as payments. (PAY-31 to PAY-36)
- **Where payment use is real.** Chainalysis counts USD 220bn of attributable cross-border stablecoin transfers in its 2026 year (monthly flows rose from USD 11bn to USD 24bn). Brazil, the US and Nigeria top its adoption index, and the average cross-border transfer is about USD 3,000. This is a vendor lower bound, not a corridor cost. (PAY-37)
- **Macro effects measured so far are small.** T-bill yields move a few basis points per USD 3.5bn of flows, and the IMF finds no clear disintermediation in El Salvador. Dollarisation, CFM circumvention and deposit substitution are well-argued mechanisms. The large numbers (USD 1tn EM deposit flight; USD 1–3tn market-cap cases) are scenarios. (PAY-41 to PAY-48)
- **UK.** The BoE dropped stablecoin holding limits in its 22 June 2026 policy statement. It replaced them with a temporary issuance guardrail (reported as £40bn per systemic coin) and 30/70 backing, with the stated aim of protecting credit supply during the transition. BIS AER 2026 Box C still shows the superseded 40/60 calibration. (PAY-49, PAY-50)
- **Alternatives are improving but unfinished.** Nexus go-live is targeted for 2027 and it is not live. Agorá ran real-value tests in July 2026 (about CHF 800k, about 80 seconds on average, not integrated with RTGS). Swift's retail scheme has been in use since June 2026 with more than 25 banks. Swift's shared ledger has no reported live volume. (PAY-23, PAY-24, PAY-26, PAY-29)
- **Highest-risk repo fixes:**
  - the USDT-vs-Visa comparison: it sets gross on-chain value against payments volume, and Visa's average ticket is about USD 55, not "a few hundred"
  - the supply trajectory, which is a year out
  - the "6–8%" US→Mexico/Philippines claim and the "<1% total" stablecoin cost claim
  - SWIFT speed given as "1–3 business days"
  - "Retail CBDC banned under GENIUS Act" (Section 4)
- **Stale status claims.** mBridge is no longer BIS-run (the BIS exited in Oct 2024). Agorá is BIS-led with eight central banks, not "led by the BIS and New York Fed". The same errors recur in `data/rails.ts` and the glossary. (PAY-29, PAY-30)
- **Not verified:**
  - the IMF paper's full text, its data window, and whether GBP/UK is among its 18 countries
  - RPW UK→India/Nigeria/Philippines/Kenya corridor costs for Q1 2026
  - McKinsey's primary page
  - Swift's primary pages (Section 6)

## 3. Fair comparison framework: a £1,000 UK-outbound payment

A comparison only works if every route is priced to the same finished outcome. The question is not "what does a token transfer cost?" but "what does it cost, and how long does it take, for a named recipient to hold usable local currency?"

### 3.1 What must be held constant

| Dimension | Hold constant as | Why it changes the answer |
|---|---|---|
| Amount | £1,000 debited from a UK current account. All routes are quoted at the same timestamp. | Costs are non-linear. The IMF gives 4.12% at USD 50 and 1.54% at USD 5,000 (PAY-04). MTO medians fall from about 200 bp at USD 200 to 40–60 bp at USD 10,000 (PAY-08). No published USD 200 or USD 500 percentage can be scaled to £1,000. |
| Payout objective | The recipient holds spendable INR, NGN, PHP or KES in a named bank account or mobile wallet. | A USD token in the recipient's wallet is an unfinished payment. The off-ramp fee, FX and time must be included, or the route is not comparable. |
| FX | Every route is measured against the same mid-market benchmark at the same moment, recording both conversions (GBP→USD token and USD token→local). | Premiums and parallel rates can make stablecoin costs look negative (PAY-02, PAY-08, PAY-11). That reflects capital controls or segmentation, may be unavailable or unlawful at scale, and is someone else's cost. |
| All fee legs | **Stablecoin route:**<br>• sender bank or card fee<br>• exchange deposit fee<br>• trading fee or spread<br>• token withdrawal or network fee<br>• bridge fee if the chains differ<br>• receiving exchange's trading fee<br>• fiat withdrawal fee<br>• recipient bank fee<br>• any T+0 redemption fee<br>**Traditional route:**<br>• sender fee and FX margin<br>• intermediary deductions (OUR/SHA/BEN)<br>• receiver fee | Network fees are under a quarter of stablecoin cost (PAY-03). Receiver-side fees (about 1.4%, plus 0.9% with FX) are invisible in sender-side metrics (PAY-19). |
| Speed definition | The clock runs from sender debit to recipient usable funds in local currency. Report first-time use (including KYC at two exchanges) and repeat use separately. | Swift's 10-minute figure stops at the beneficiary bank (PAY-22). End-to-end wholesale is 57% within one hour globally, 28.8% for Asia-Pacific and 26% for Africa (PAY-20). Token finality is not payout. |
| Operating hours | Test on a weekday, an evening, and a weekend or holiday. Check UK Faster Payments, the destination's instant system and the exchange's fiat withdrawal hours. | A 24/7 token leg only helps if both fiat ramps are also open (PAY-13). |
| Reachability and limits | Same recipient, same payout instrument, same KYC tier, same limits. | Exchanges may not serve the recipient's country or bank. Few stablecoin–fiat pairs have depth (PAY-10). |
| Protection and compliance | Record each provider's regulatory status, reversibility, sanctions/travel-rule handling and consumer redress. | Not a price line, but a risk committee will ask. UK APP-fraud reimbursement applies to Faster Payments, not to on-chain transfers [Likely; check PSR scope before publishing]. |

### 3.3 Minimum cost stack to publish

If the site shows a worked £1,000 example, publish the stack, not just a headline percentage. For each route, show:

1. amount sent
2. each fee line
3. FX applied at each conversion versus the mid-market rate at the timestamp
4. amount received in local currency
5. time to usable funds
6. the hours tested
7. the source and date of each number

Label any figure that is illustrative rather than observed. Without a dated, observed UK-outbound quote set, the honest statement is directional: the token leg costs cents, while the ramps, FX and compliance set the price.

## 5. Contradictions, myths and open questions

- **One estimate, three logos.** The USD 390bn "genuine payments" figure appears as McKinsey/Artemis (early 2026), as BIS AER 2026 fn 29 and as "BIS estimates" in the IMF speech. Treat it as one vendor estimate until the BIS papers' own methodology is checked (PAY-32 to PAY-34).
- **Gross volume has no single number.** Estimates are USD 28tn (BIS), more than USD 30tn (IMF speech) and about USD 35tn (McKinsey), and Visa/Allium adjusted figures differ again. The site should never quote gross volume without naming provider, filter and date.
- **Cross-border stablecoin flows differ by about 28x between sources.** The IMF speech and coverage of the IMF paper give USD 6.1–6.3tn of 2025 "cross-border" volume (unnamed sources). Chainalysis counts USD 220bn of attributable cross-border transfers. The definitions (exchange-attributed flows versus person or business transfers) are not reconciled anywhere I could read.
- **Myth: "stablecoins cost under 1%".** That is the network fee. The all-in average is about 2% at USD 200 under the best-case ramp choice (PAY-02 to PAY-04).
- **Myth: negative cost means savings.** It reflects stablecoin premiums and capital controls. It may not be lawful or available at scale, and the premium is paid by someone on the other side (PAY-03, PAY-08).
- **Myth pair: "SWIFT takes 1–3 days" and "Swift is now instant".** Both are wrong. Interbank arrival is mostly minutes, while end-to-end crediting within one hour is 57% globally and below 30% in Asia-Pacific and Africa (PAY-20, PAY-22).
- **The RPW figure in Q1 2026 is 6.2% or 5.7%.** Do not present the fall to 5.7% as an improvement. The FSB attributes it entirely to rebasing (PAY-14).
- **RPW Q3 2025 secondaries conflict.** They disagree on the bank average (14.99% vs 11.15%) and on the publication date (Sep 2025 vs Apr 2026). The RPW primary was not reachable (PAY-15).
- **The IMF paper has internal comparability issues.** The 33-corridor subset (traditional 5.81% vs stablecoin 1.02%) is not representative of the full sample (2.18%), and the data window and country list were not visible (PAY-05).
- **T-bill effect sizes vary by version.** BIS WP 1270's current abstract gives 0.71 bp on impact and up to about 4 bp in 10 days; the AER 2025 summary gave 2.5–5 bp (PAY-46).
- **UK backing rules.** BIS AER 2026 Box C (23 Jun) still shows the 40/60 calibration the BoE replaced on 22 Jun (PAY-49, PAY-50).
- **Swift network size.** Swift's own 2026 material says 11,500 institutions and FSB 2026 says 12,500 (PAY-25).
- **Agorá count.** The BIS cites seven jurisdictions for the legal-finality finding but eight central banks once the Bank of Canada is included. NY Fed participation is contested by commentators under EO 14178, and no official reconciliation was found (PAY-29, PAY-53).
- **Retail speed KPI.** It fell from 74% (2023) to 59.8% within one day, but it uses providers' stated times, not measured execution. Do not cite it as observed slowing (PAY-20).
