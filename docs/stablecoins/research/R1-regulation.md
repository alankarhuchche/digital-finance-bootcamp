- **Confidence.** High means I read it in a primary source. Med means I read it only in part of a primary source, or in a reputable secondary source such as a law-firm briefing or trade press. Low means it rests on a search-result snippet, a single secondary source or my memory.
- **"Memory"** in the Accessed column means the fact is from before June 2026 and was not re-fetched in this run. Every post-June 2026 fact comes from a live source. Where no source could be found, the gap is labelled as unverified.

## 1. Editor summary

1. **No part of the UK stablecoin regime is in force yet.** The FSMA 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/102) were made on 4 Feb 2026. The FCA's final rules (PS26/9 to PS26/13) were published on 30 Jun 2026. Both apply from **25 Oct 2027**. The FCA's application window for the savings provisions runs from 30 Sep 2026 to 28 Feb 2027 (REG-13, REG-26, REG-27).
2. **The BoE systemic regime is final policy plus draft rules, not rules in force.** The 22 Jun 2026 policy statement dropped the £20,000 individual and £10m business holding limits. In their place is a **temporary £40bn issuance guardrail per systemic stablecoin**. The backing-asset split moved from 60/40 to **70/30**: short-term gilts of 6 months or less, and unremunerated BoE deposits. Issuers can hold up to 95% in gilts while they scale. The draft Code of Practice consultation closed on 22 Sep 2026, and the BoE intends to finalise it by end-2026 (REG-05 to REG-07).
3. **The FCA regulates every UK-issued qualifying stablecoin, in any currency.** The BoE joins only after HM Treasury recognises an issuer as systemic. I found no recognition to date (REG-11, REG-12).
4. **The payments perimeter is being redrawn while the crypto regime waits to start.** A draft amending SI was laid around 15 Sep 2026. It takes transfers and exchanges of *UK-issued* qualifying stablecoins out of dealing and arranging. It also excludes holding them temporarily to execute a payment from the safeguarding activity. A Commons committee considered it on 11 Nov 2026. Lords approval has been reported but is not confirmed, and the SI is not yet confirmed as made. **Overseas coins such as USDT and USDC stay inside the dealing and arranging perimeter** (REG-17 to REG-19).
5. **HMT's July 2026 payments consultation would move UK-issued stablecoin payments into a reformed payments regime.** Overseas coins would follow only if HMT determines that their home regime gives similar outcomes. The response is expected by end-2026, followed by roughly two years of legislative and rulebook work (REG-20).
6. **Banks face a PRA constraint.** The PRA's 18 May 2026 letter says a deposit-taker should offer retail digital money only as deposits. It should issue stablecoins or e-money only from an **insolvency-remote, distinctly branded entity**, and wholesale-only issuance is treated as lower risk. A companion letter keeps an interim prudential approach for cryptoasset exposures until at least 2028 (REG-33, REG-34; both via a secondary source because the PDF is encrypted).
7. **Tokenised deposits sit outside the crypto regime by inference, not by name.** SI 2026/102 never uses the term. The repo's line that tokenised deposits were "confirmed as deposits in law" in February 2026 overstates what the SI does (REG-15).
8. **US: the GENIUS Act takes effect on 18 Jan 2027.** Only the OCC has a final rule (21 Oct 2026). The Treasury, FDIC, Federal Reserve, NCUA and FinCEN rules are still proposals or interim rules, so most agencies missed the 18 Jul 2026 statutory deadline (REG-40 to REG-42).
9. **The US yield question is unresolved.** The statute bars *issuers* from paying interest. The OCC dropped its proposed presumption against affiliate and exchange rewards and will judge them case by case. The CLARITY Act, which carried the Tillis–Alsobrooks yield compromise, failed cloture on 15 Sep 2026 (REG-37, REG-41, REG-44, REG-45).
10. **No foreign regime, the UK included, has a GENIUS comparability determination.** The ban on platforms offering non-permitted coins in the US starts on 18 Jul 2028 (REG-39, REG-43).
11. **EU: MiCA's CASP grandfathering ended on 1 Jul 2026.** As at 1 Sep 2026 there were 39 EMTs and no authorised ARTs. Multi-issuance is contested. Commission services say MiCA "does not currently prohibit" it, the EBA wants a stronger framework, and secondary reports say the ECB and ESRB want it restricted. No legislative proposal exists yet (REG-46 to REG-51).
12. **The digital euro is in trilogue.** Parliament's mandate was confirmed on 9 Jul 2026. There was no agreed text at 18 Nov 2026, and the next trilogue was set for 18 Nov (REG-53).
13. **FATF (July 2026):** 83% of responding jurisdictions (91 of 109) have Travel Rule legislation, but 60% of those (55 of 91) have taken no Travel Rule supervisory action. The FATF's own executive summary says "almost half", which contradicts that figure. The update treats stablecoins as a cross-crime risk and expects issuers to be able to freeze tokens (REG-55 to REG-58).
14. **Global bodies:** the FSB's October 2025 peer review found implementation "incomplete, uneven and inconsistent". The FSB's October 2026 note to the G20 covers cross-border stablecoin vulnerabilities. The BIS 2026 Annual Economic Report says stablecoins "fall short" as money. The BCBS review of its crypto standard has no published outcome yet (REG-61 to REG-65).
15. **Repo:** `14-regulation.ts` is the most stale file. It calls the FCA "principles-based", dates the financial promotions regime wrongly, still shows the July 2026 MiCA deadline as upcoming, misstates the GENIUS yield ban, refers to SEC "enforcement" against Uniswap and Lido, and describes Hong Kong's 2024 sandbox as current. `26-gbtd.ts` is mostly current but overstates the legal position of tokenised deposits and calls the £40bn guardrail "per coin" (Section 4).

## 3. What a UK bank can and cannot do (November 2026), status-qualified

## 5. Contradictions, common myths and open questions

**Contradictions found in sources**

- **FATF:** the July 2026 executive summary says "almost half" of jurisdictions with Travel Rule laws have taken no supervisory action. The body says 60% (55 of 91), which is well over half. Cite the body figure (REG-55).
- **FCA PS26/10 redemption deadline:** A&O Shearman says the T+1 clock starts after KYC. Another law-firm summary in the search results says "24 hours". The 24-hour figure may be the BoE systemic rule (REG-08) being read across. Check the PS26/10 text (REG-28).
- **BoE start date:** the BoE news release says regulated stablecoins can operate "from 2027" in a garbled sentence. The FCA regime starts on 25 Oct 2027, while the BoE CoP applies only after an issuer is recognised. Do not write "the UK regime starts in 2027" without saying which part (REG-07, REG-13).
- **EU multi-issuance:** Commission services say MiCA "does not currently prohibit" it. The EBA calls the risks "significant to very significant". The ECB, ESCB and ESRB push for restriction, per secondary reports, while Parliament reportedly voted for multi-issuance. Treat the position as contested, not settled (REG-49 to REG-51).
- **HMT dates:** the Economic Secretary's 6 Oct 2026 speech page mentions DIGIT lead managers that HMT announced on 2 Nov 2026. The page has probably been updated, so cite the 2 Nov press release for DIGIT (REG-22, REG-23).
- **Repo internal:** `06-stablecoins.ts` gives a direct-redemption minimum of "$100K" on line 20 and "$100" on line 57.

**Common myths to avoid in the rebuilt topic**

- "The UK has a stablecoin regime." Not yet: law made, rules final, start date 25 Oct 2027 (REG-13, REG-26).
- "The BoE caps what you can hold at £20,000." That limit was dropped in June 2026. A £40bn issuance guardrail per *systemic* coin replaces it (REG-06).
- "Sterling stablecoins must be backed 100% by BoE deposits." That was the 2023 idea. The 2026 policy is 70/30, with up to 95% gilts while an issuer scales (REG-05).
- "The GENIUS Act is in force" or "it bans stablecoin yield." It takes effect on 18 Jan 2027, and it bars issuer-paid interest only (REG-37, REG-40).
- "USDC and USDT can be used freely for UK payments once the regime starts." Overseas coins stay within the dealing and arranging perimeter, and admission to the payments regime is only a proposal (REG-17, REG-20).
- "Tokenised deposits are a kind of stablecoin." In UK law they stay deposits, outside the qualifying-stablecoin definition (REG-15).
- "MiCA banned USDT." MiCA restricts which tokens EU CASPs may offer or admit to trading. It does not ban holding them (REG-46).
